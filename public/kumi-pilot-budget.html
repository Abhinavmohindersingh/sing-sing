    Kumi — Grade 3 pilot in one school

    Colours show what each tier adds: Tier 1 Tier 2 Tier 3

    This is the cost to build Kumi and run it with one Grade 3 class for one school term (10 weeks).

    Kumi records every lesson and answer. This data is used for:

    1. Progress tracking: every child’s answers and progress are recorded.
    2. Better content: Kumi finds what works and improves the rest.
    3. Teacher support: teachers see who is doing well and who needs help.
    4. Evidence: results for the PNG Government, DFAT, UNICEF and investors.

    Kumi’s AI is used during the build; children’s data never goes to an AI service. No hardware to buy.

    Assumptions and exclusions

    Assumptions
    • The pilot school is in Port Moresby (National Capital District). A school outside the capital adds domestic flights and higher security costs.
    • The pilot school and a comparison class are signed up in Phase 1. The school already has a desktop lab with desktops, internet and power. One Grade 3 class of about 30–40 students.
    • 20 lessons: 2 subjects (numeracy and literacy) × 10 weeks, one one-hour lesson per subject per week, or 2 hours per child per week.
    • PNG school terms are about 10 weeks [source]. The pilot starts at the start of a term. 2027 dates are confirmed with the school; the 2027 general election may move them.
    • The school gives the pilot class lab time for 2 hours per child each week, and the teacher’s time to run the sessions.
    • One headset per child (up to 40), labelled with their name, plus 4 spares.
    • About 55,000 words translated into Tok Pisin by AI, with the linguist verifying at around 7,000 words a day.
    • Kumi’s narration is made with ElevenLabs during the build and checked by the linguist. Children answer by tap.
    • Lessons, narration and help lines are stored on the computer, so everything works offline.
    • The pilot school has a working internet connection for most lessons. Offline mode covers outages, not a school with no connection.
    • School desktops have at least 8 GB of RAM, enough to run the lessons and narration.
    • Phase 1 is run remotely, except the school agreement and site survey, which the local linguist handles in Port Moresby.
    • The app is built on the existing Kumi prototype code (React), packaged as a Windows app.
    • Claude is used only during the build, to draft lessons and help lines. No child data is sent to an AI service.
    • The team is named before the contract is signed. All work is kept in shared repositories with documentation, so a replacement can pick it up if someone leaves.
    • The installer travels on the right PNG visa for business. Local team members and speakers are paid in kina.
    • Pre/post tests run inside the app, supervised by the teacher.
    • A core team of 6, plus a local Grade 3 teacher, carries the full workload.

    • School computers, electricity and internet.
    • Personal development lessons and a 3rd hour each week (in Tier 3).

    Assumptions
    • The pilot school is in Port Moresby (National Capital District). A school outside the capital adds domestic flights and higher security costs.
    • The pilot school and a comparison class are signed up in Phase 1. The school already has a desktop lab with desktops, internet and power. One Grade 3 class of about 30–40 students.

    • PNG school terms are about 10 weeks [source]. The pilot starts at the start of a term. 2027 dates are confirmed with the school; the 2027 general election may move them.
    • The school gives the pilot class lab time for 2 hours per child each week, and the teacher’s time to run the sessions.
    • One headset per child (up to 40), labelled with their name, plus 4 spares.
    • About 55,000 words translated into Tok Pisin by AI, with the linguist verifying at around 7,000 words a day.
    • Kumi’s Tok Pisin voice is built from about 6 hours of a voice actor’s recordings. English answers use an off-the-shelf speech model; Tok Pisin answers are by tap.
    • Speech recognition and Kumi’s voice run on the school computer, so voice works offline. Offline, Kumi helps from approved lines; open conversation needs the internet.
    • The pilot school has a working internet connection for most lessons. Offline mode covers outages, not a school with no connection.
    • School desktops have at least 8 GB of RAM, enough to run Kumi’s speech recognition and voice alongside the lessons.
    • Phase 1 is run remotely, except the school agreement and site survey, which the local linguist handles in Port Moresby.
    • The app is built on the existing Kumi prototype code (React), packaged as a Windows app.
    • Claude is used through Amazon Bedrock’s Australian region, so AI requests are processed in Australia [source]. The English voice service (ElevenLabs) is overseas; parents are told in the consent form.
    • The team is named before the contract is signed. All work is kept in shared repositories with documentation, so a replacement can pick it up if someone leaves.
    • The installer travels on the right PNG visa for business. Local team members and speakers are paid in kina.
    • Pre/post tests run inside the app, supervised by the teacher.

    Assumptions and exclusions

    Assumptions
    • The pilot school is in Port Moresby (National Capital District). A school outside the capital adds domestic flights and higher security costs.
    • The pilot school and a comparison class are signed up in Phase 1. The school already has a desktop lab with desktops, internet and power. One Grade 3 class of about 30–40 students.
    • 30 lessons: 3 subjects × 10 weeks, one one-hour lesson per subject per week, or 3 hours per child per week.
    • PNG school terms are about 10 weeks [source]. The pilot starts at the start of a term. 2027 dates are confirmed with the school; the 2027 general election may move them.
    • The school gives the pilot class lab time for 3 hours per child each week, and the teacher’s time to run the sessions.
    • One headset per child (up to 40), labelled with their name, plus 4 spares.
    • About 80,000 words translated into Tok Pisin by AI, with the linguist verifying at around 7,000 words a day.
    • Tok Pisin speech is built from about 30 hours of scripted recordings by 20 adult speakers, and 6 hours from Kumi’s voice actor. Recognition is built for what the lessons need (answers, numbers, words and short requests); open conversation in Tok Pisin improves through the pilot.
    • Speech recognition and Kumi’s voice run on the school computer, so voice works offline. Offline, Kumi helps from approved lines; open conversation needs the internet.
    • The pilot school has a working internet connection for most lessons. Offline mode covers outages, not a school with no connection.
    • School desktops have at least 8 GB of RAM, enough to run Kumi’s speech recognition and voice alongside the lessons.
    • Phase 1 is run remotely, except the school agreement and site survey, which the local linguist handles in Port Moresby.
    • The app is built on the existing Kumi prototype code (React), packaged as a Windows app.
    • Claude is used through Amazon Bedrock’s Australian region, so AI requests are processed in Australia [source]. The English voice service (ElevenLabs) is overseas; parents are told in the consent form.
    • The team is named before the contract is signed. All work is kept in shared repositories with documentation, so a replacement can pick it up if someone leaves.
    • The installer travels on the right PNG visa for business. Local team members and speakers are paid in kina.
    • Pre/post tests run inside the app, supervised by the teacher.
    • A core team of 7, plus a local Grade 3 teacher and an external education specialist, carries the full workload.
    Not included
    • School computers, electricity and internet.
    • The optional add-ons above: Mac build, on-device AI model for schools without internet.
    • Native iPhone or Android apps.
    • Grades 4–6 content (about $1,980 per lesson to add).
    • Training a language model from scratch, or owning AI hardware.
    • Running costs after the 3-month bridge (shown above, funded separately).
    • GST.
