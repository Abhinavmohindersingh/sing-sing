How Kumi works

  1. Desktop application. Runs on the Windows computers the school already has. Installs from a USB stick and updates itself when online.
  2. Online and offline support. Pick one or both: Online Online Works while the school has internet. Every answer goes straight to the cloud, lessons load from the cloud, and the teacher screen and live AI help work. Offline Works offline Works with no internet. Lessons, questions and student login run on the computer, Kumi still talks from saved lines, and everything is saved on the computer first, so a power cut loses nothing. Offline only (without Online): Claude, the English voice (ElevenLabs), the teacher screen and the weekly AI check need the internet, so they are locked. Our own AI model and Tok Pisin speech work offline, if you want them. Both Works either way. Saved work is sent by itself when the internet is back, with nothing lost and nothing doubled. Kumi switches between live and saved help by itself.
  3. Individual student login and profiles. Every student has their own profile and signs in with a picture and a PIN. Their progress is kept in their profile, and one student cannot open another student’s work.
  4. Lessons. 20 one-hour lessons with pictures, animations and practice questions, in English and Tok Pisin. Pick the subjects (numeracy, literacy, personal development) and set the hours for each in the left panel; the cost follows the hours. If the content is provided to us, our designer turns it into animated lessons. If we have to prepare the content ourselves, that is a separate tick with its own cost.
  5. AI content curation. Every child gets the same lessons, but Kumi watches every answer and chooses what comes next. Mary gets three answers wrong, so she gets easier questions and a hint. Dura answers fast, so he gets harder ones. Two ways to run it (pick one): Way 1: Claude AI Online Best quality answers, processed in Australia. Needs the internet, so it needs the Online option; offline, Kumi uses its saved help. Monthly usage cost. Way 2: our own AI model Works offline Runs on one GPU server in the school lab, which the computers reach over the lab network, so the school’s computers stay as they are. No usage cost. Simpler answers than Claude. If the server is down, Kumi uses its saved help.
  6. AI speech. Pick the languages first (English, Tok Pisin, or both), then the mode. The mode applies to every language ticked: English and two-way means two-way in English; both languages and two-way means two-way in both. One-way Kumi talks to the child. The child taps Kumi, or says a keyword like “help” (halivim), “again” (gen) or “next” (nekis), and Kumi explains the step out loud. The child answers by tapping. Two-way A real conversation, like talking to a teacher: the child speaks any question about the lesson and Kumi answers out loud. English: ElevenLabs English A natural English voice from a cloud service, so it needs the Online option. Monthly plan. Tok Pisin: our own speech models Tok Pisin Works offline We found no commercial Tok Pisin voice, so we build Kumi’s own (Piper, MIT licence, trained on our voice actor’s recordings). For two-way, listening starts from Meta’s open Omnilingual ASR, which already covers Tok Pisin, and is trained further on children’s speech. ElevenLabs is never used for Tok Pisin. No per-minute cost.
  7. Teacher screen. See how the whole class is doing and which students need help.
